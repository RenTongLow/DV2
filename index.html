Overview

Welcome to the Australian Running Journey

An interactive exploration into national fitness trends, demographics, and race-day culture across Australia.

Over the past decade, running has evolved from a solitary workout into a cornerstone of Australian physical culture. Driven by shifts in public health awareness, lifestyle adaptations, and community event expansions, millions of Australians regularly lace up their running shoes. This data visualisation dashboard investigates the phenomenon across four key dimensions: how participation has scaled over time, who comprises the Australian running community, where runners and events are geographically concentrated, and how runners transition from casual 5Ks to full marathons.

01 / Running as a Lifestyle

Running as a Lifestyle

How national running participation evolved and how it compares to alternative fitness choices.

Key finding

Running has grown from under 3 million participants in 2015 to over 4 million, with a spike in 2020, a dip in 2021–22, and new highs in 2024–25 (the survey moved online in 2023, so the later rise is not strictly like-for-like).

Running Participation Over Time

How has national running participation evolved across survey years?

Running participation in Australia has increased from under 3 million in 2015 to over 4 million. The participation rate peaked at 17.8% in 2020, fell back to 15.8% by 2022, then climbed to a new high of 18.0% in 2025. The 2023 survey change from phone to online panel may account for part of that recovery.

Running vs Other Activities

How does running compare to alternative fitness activities?

Running is Australia's fourth most popular activity (16.5%), behind walking (50.2%), gym workouts (34.1%) and bush walking (17.5%), and just ahead of swimming (15.4%).

When Are Running Events Held in Australia? (2025)

Number of scheduled running events by day across each month

Events cluster on weekends (87% fall on a Saturday or Sunday) and in autumn, with March the busiest month. The busiest single day is Sunday 4 May, with 16 events.

02 / Who Runs?

Who Runs?

Analyzing running frequency distribution, gender balance, and age cohort demographics.

Key finding

Running is an inclusive activity. Men make up a slight majority of runners (54.5% vs 45.5%), and participation peaks among 25–34-year-olds (31.8%).

Running Frequency Distribution

How frequently do different age cohorts engage in running?

Under-35s most often run three times a week, while runners aged 35 and over most often run twice. Running five or more times a week is most common among 18–24-year-olds (16%).

Participant Gender Composition

What is the gender balance among active runners?

Men account for 54.5% of runners and women for 45.5%, a gap of nine percentage points.

Participation by Age Group

Which age demographics record the highest runner engagement?

Participation peaks at 31.8% among 25–34-year-olds and falls to 12.4% for those 55 and over. Identifying these age clusters helps target community fitness initiatives and structured running programs effectively.

03 / Where Do People Run?

Where Do People Run?

State participation rates, absolute runner volumes, and the geographic dispersion of events.

Key finding

Volume and rate tell different stories. New South Wales and Victoria have the most runners in absolute terms, while the ACT (29.4%), Victoria (27.1%) and Western Australia (26.2%) have the highest participation rates.

Running Participation Rates by State

How do state participation rates scale relative to population?

Participation ranges from 19.8% in the Northern Territory to 29.4% in the ACT. Victoria (27.1%) and Western Australia (26.2%) follow closely behind the ACT.

Total Runner Population Volume

Where are absolute runner volumes concentrated nationally?

New South Wales and Victoria house the largest absolute volume of runners owing to their major urban centers, although the ACT, one of the smallest jurisdictions, has the highest participation rate.

Geographic Distribution of Running Events

How are race-day events geographically dispersed across Australia?

Events cluster around the east coast and the major cities. Victoria, NSW and Queensland host about two-thirds of listed events, with a few remote races on Norfolk Island and Christmas Island.

04 / From 5K to Marathon

From 5K to Marathon

Exploring race category popularity, historical trend shifts, and completion-time distributions.

Key finding

Shorter races are the gateway. 5K and 10K events attract the most participants, while marathons show the widest spread of finish times.

Participation by Distance

Which standard race categories attract the most participants?

Shorter distances like 5K and 10K dominate participant numbers, serving as welcoming entry points for everyday runners and charity fundraisers.

Distance Popularity Ranking

How have distance category popularity rankings shifted over time?

Rankings are largely stable: the 5K is first every year and the 10K second in all years but 2025. The half marathon overtook the full marathon in 2023, reached second place in 2025, and fell back to fourth in 2026.

Completion-Time Distribution

How do finish times vary across different race distances?

Finish times scale naturally with distance. Marathons exhibit a wider dispersion of finish times due to diverse pacing strategies and endurance levels among participants.

Conclusion

Running Is Part of How Australia Stays Active

Final insights into Australia's active running community.

  • Long-run Growth: Participation rose from 14.8% in 2015 to 18.0% in 2025, despite a dip after the 2020 peak.
  • Broad Appeal: Both men and women run, though participation is highest among 25–34-year-olds and drops sharply after 55.
  • Volume vs. Rate: The most populous states have the most runners, but the ACT has the highest participation rate.
  • Event Accessibility: Short races like 5K and 10K are good starting points for new runners.
