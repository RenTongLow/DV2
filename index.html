Overview

Welcome to the Australian Running Journey

An interactive exploration into national fitness trends, demographics, and race-day culture across Australia.

Over the past decade, running has evolved from a solitary workout into a cornerstone of Australian physical culture. Driven by shifts in public health awareness, lifestyle adaptations, and community event expansions, millions of Australians regularly lace up their running shoes. This data visualisation dashboard investigates the phenomenon across four key dimensions: how participation has scaled over time, who comprises the Australian running community, where runners and events are geographically concentrated, and how runners transition from casual 5Ks to full marathons.

01 / Running as a Lifestyle

Running as a Lifestyle

How national running participation evolved and how it compares to alternative fitness choices.

Key finding

Running participation has grown from under 3 million participants in 2015 to over 4 million. Running ranked fourth among popular physical activities at 16.5%. Running events also favour weekends, with 87% held on Saturdays and Sundays.

Running Participation Over Time

How has national running participation evolved across survey years?

Running participation in Australia has increased from under 3 million in 2015 to over 4 million. The participation rate peaked at 3.68 million in 2020, fell back to 3.35 million by 2021, then climbed to a new high of 4.1 million in 2025. The 2023 survey change from phone to online panel may account for part of that recovery.

Running vs Other Activities

How does running compare to alternative fitness activities?

Running is Australia's fourth most popular activity (16.5%), behind walking (50.2%), gym workouts (34.1%) and bush walking (17.5%), and just ahead of swimming (15.4%).

When Are Running Events Held in Australia? (2025)

Number of scheduled running events by day across each month

Marathon events cluster on weekends, with 87% held on Saturdays or Sundays, and are particularly concentrated in autumn. March is the busiest month overall, while Sunday, 4 May, records the highest number of events on a single day, with 16 events. This pattern suggests that organisers favour weekends and certain times of the year to accommodate participants.

02 / Who Runs?

Who Runs?

Analyzing running frequency distribution, gender balance, and age cohort demographics.

Key finding

Running is an inclusive activity. Men make up a slight majority of runners, and participation peaks among 25–34 year-olds.

Running Frequency Distribution

How frequently do different age cohorts engage in running?

Running frequency varies by age. Runners under 35 most commonly run three times a week, while those aged 35 and over tend to run twice a week. The 18–24 age group shows the strongest commitment to frequent running, with 16% running five or more times per week.

Participant Gender Composition

What is the gender balance among active runners?

Running participation in Australia is relatively balanced between men and women, highlighting its broad appeal across genders. The waffle chart makes this comparison easy to see, with each square representing an equal share of participants.

Participation by Age Group

Which age demographics record the highest runner engagement?

Running participation is highest among adults aged 25–34, at 31.8%, but drops to 12.4% among those aged 55 and over. This highlights a clear age difference in running participation, with younger and middle-aged adults more likely to run than older adults.

03 / Where Do People Run?

Where Do People Run?

State participation rates, absolute runner volumes, and the geographic dispersion of events.

Key finding

Volume and rate tell different stories. New South Wales and Victoria have the largest numbers of runners, while the ACT (29.4%), Victoria (27.1%) and Western Australia (26.2%) lead in participation rates. This contrast shows that the states with the most runners are not necessarily those with the highest proportion of residents who run.

Running Participation Rates by State

How do state participation rates scale relative to population?

The ACT records the highest running participation rate at 29.4%, followed by Victoria (27.1%) and Western Australia (26.2%). The Northern Territory has the lowest rate at 19.8%. These differences reveal where running is most common relative to each state's population.

Total Runner Population Volume

Where are absolute runner volumes concentrated nationally?

New South Wales and Victoria have the largest runner populations, reflecting their larger populations. Unlike participation rates, total runner counts show where the greatest numbers of runners are concentrated, rather than how common running is among residents.

Geographic Distribution of Running Events

How are race-day events geographically dispersed across Australia?

Running events are concentrated along Australia's east coast and around major cities. Victoria, New South Wales and Queensland host approximately two-thirds of the listed events, while a small number take place in remote locations, including Norfolk Island and Christmas Island.

04 / From 5K to Marathon

From 5K to Marathon

Exploring race category popularity, historical trend shifts, and completion-time distributions.

Key finding

Shorter distances attract more participants, while longer races present greater performance variation. The 5K and 10K remain popular choices, whereas marathon finish times vary more widely, reflecting differences in runners' pace and endurance.

Participation by Distance

Which standard race categories attract the most participants?

The 5K and 10K attract the most participants, making shorter races popular choices for recreational runners and those new to organised racing. Longer distances, such as half marathons and full marathons, appeal to runners seeking greater endurance challenges.

Distance Popularity Ranking

How have distance category popularity rankings shifted over time?

The 5K consistently ranks first, while the 10K generally holds second place. The half marathon overtook the full marathon in 2023 and rose to second place in 2025, before dropping to fourth in 2026. Despite these changes, shorter distances remain popular overall.

Completion-Time Distribution

How do finish times vary across different race distances?

Completion times increase with race distance, with full marathons showing the widest spread of finish times. This greater variation reflects differences in runners' pace, endurance and race strategies. The median and spread of each distance also help compare typical performance across race categories.

Conclusion

Running Is Part of How Australia Stays Active

Key insights into Australia's running participation, demographics, locations and events.

  • Participation Trend: Running participation increased from under 3 million participants in 2015 to over 4 million in 2025.
  • Age and Gender: Running attracts both men and women, with participation highest among 25–34 year-olds and lower among adults aged 55 and over.
  • Where People Run: New South Wales and Victoria have the largest runner populations, while the ACT has the highest participation rate.
  • Events and Distances: 5K and 10K races attract the most participants, and 87% of listed running events take place on weekends.
